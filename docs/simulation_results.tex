\subsection{Simulation transfer protocol}
We freeze one successful scripted Franka Panda demonstration on a single box
and reuse its labels in every evaluation cell. The source trajectory is selected
in development and includes a 0.10\,m hover and no commanded carry rotation.
Evaluation changes the target object, gripper and physical pickup/shelf
configuration. Five grippers (Panda, Robotiq 2F-85, Robotiq 2F-140, XArm and
Rethink) remain mounted on a Panda arm. The four target assets are the YCB
pudding box, gelatin box, foam brick and potted-meat can. These are rigid scan
meshes with convex collision decompositions and the repository's configured
masses; neither mesh scale nor contact parameters are adjusted during evaluation.

The predeclared grid contains $5\times4\times6=120$ cells per executor.
Six fresh configurations are drawn by stratifying pickup $x$, $y$ and yaw and
shelf translation $x$, $y$ and $z$. Pickup coordinates span
$[-0.23,-0.21]\times[-0.14,-0.10]$\,m, with yaw in $[50,65]$ degrees.
Shelf translation spans $[-0.03,0]\times[-0.04,0.04]\times[-0.03,0]$\,m.
Each configuration is crossed with every object and gripper. The destination
is the middle slot of the upper cubby shelf, whose physical location changes
with the shelf translation. The object rest orientations and this local
workspace are development choices. All objects settle under physics before
perception. This protocol measures transfer within that specified domain.

The pretrained diffusion grasp generator and target keypoints use the full YCB surface scan transformed
by the simulator object pose. Obstacle checking uses fused camera observations;
the retained legacy place-zone filter also uses simulator geometry. The nominal
robot configuration seeds numerical inverse kinematics, while the simulated arm
starts in its ordinary physical home state. The scheduled path checks include the grasp, release and final waypoints;
other path samples have stride four. Checking stops at the first rejection,
and legacy fallback may execute a rejected candidate whose remaining waypoints
were not checked. Fallback executions are retained and reported separately. These discrete checks provide diagnostic evidence rather than a
continuous collision guarantee.

The primary executor refits a Gaussian process to the transported labels,
queries its reference state and phase, and tracks its predicted reference and
orientation under joint-position control. Measured tracking error gates phase
progress at a 30\,mm tolerance, with a phase-speed factor of 0.25 and a
2400-step budget. The controller gains are fixed, so this experiment evaluates
pose and gripper transfer. Direct replay of the same transported labels is the
paired execution baseline. At 20\,Hz it holds each of the 240 label poses for
eight control steps and requires four consecutive opposing-finger contact
samples at grasp acquisition. The GP executor advances after its first such
contact sample, subject to its tracking gate. This comparison therefore includes
differences in timing and grasp confirmation as well as policy refitting.
Both executors use the identical source, target
scenes and cached grasp draws. Candidate, effective-pose and transported-label
hashes check these identities.

Success retains the repository's placement criterion: horizontal object-origin
error below 60\,mm and the lowest object-geometry point within 30\,mm of the
shelf surface. We additionally hold the final arm configuration with open jaws
for two seconds. Throughout the final second, the object must satisfy the slot
criterion, touch the destination board, have no robot contact, and move within
a 2\,mm position span. This check can only downgrade an execution outcome.
All scheduled cells enter the denominator, including refusals, setup errors and
execution failures. The study uses MuJoCo 3.7.0 and robosuite 1.5.2, with serial
CPU physics. Planner draws are saved in a dedicated cache; the environment seed
is not treated as a seed for the external grasp generator.

\subsection{Development evidence}
A 55-cell development comparison of source trajectories increased position
placement from $39/55$ with the original source to $41/55$ with the compact
source; seven cases improved and five regressed with identical candidate
caches. The nominal IK seed and explicit task-waypoint checks subsequently
achieved $48/55$ with both replay and the refitted GP executor, including the
post-release settling criterion. All 55 executor pairs had identical candidate
caches, effective grasp poses and transported-label hashes, and identical binary
outcomes. These development cells are excluded from the prospective evaluation.

\subsection{Prospective evaluation}
The primary GP-reference executor succeeds in $111/120$ scheduled cells
($92.5\%$). All 120 cells execute: nine fail placement, with no refusals,
setup errors or diagnostic errors. Conditional execution success therefore
also equals $92.5\%$. Per-gripper counts are $23/24$ for Panda, $22/24$ for
Robotiq 2F-85, $23/24$ for Robotiq 2F-140, $24/24$ for XArm and $19/24$ for
Rethink. Per-object counts are $29/30$ for pudding, $28/30$ for gelatin,
$28/30$ for the brick and $26/30$ for meat. Across the six shared configurations,
success ranges from $17/20$ to $20/20$.

Seven cells execute a legacy path fallback, of which four succeed. The remaining
113 cells succeed in 107 cases. These results include fallback execution and
must not be described as a strict-feasibility success rate. Among successful
placements, final horizontal error has a median of 5.7\,mm and a 95th percentile
of 14.8\,mm; the largest is 54.2\,mm, within the declared 60\,mm criterion.
These are final supported, released placement errors, conditional on success.
Ten successful primary trials and fourteen successful replay trials include
recorded robot/gripper contact with the table or shelf during execution. Thus placement success does not imply an
obstacle-contact-free trajectory; contact traces are reported separately.

The paired replay baseline succeeds in $113/120$ cells ($94.2\%$), with seven
execution failures and no refusals or setup/diagnostic errors. All 120 pairs
match in source, candidate-cache hash, effective grasp pose, transported-label
hash and initial scene. Both methods succeed in 110 cells and fail in six;
replay succeeds in three GP failures and fails in one GP success. These paired
results do not show a success-rate advantage for the GP-reference executor.
The three replay-only successes correspond to GP tracking stalls, whereas the
GP-only success is a Rethink/brick case. Replay's successful-placement XY error
has a median of 6.4\,mm and a 95th percentile of 22.9\,mm. Neither these conditional
error summaries nor the two-cell difference in success counts establish
statistical superiority on a broader task distribution.

\subsection{Failure analysis}
Thirty rendered normal-selection reproductions cover all ten case pairs with a
failure in either executor and five diverse success controls. All reproduce
their original outcome and pinned inputs. Inspection of synchronized overview
and side-camera sequences, together with control-step traces, separates three
GP tracking stalls recovered by replay from failures shared by both methods.
One Panda/gelatin fallback never acquires the object. Two Robotiq 2F-85
fallbacks stall after lifting; one has recorded arm-to-shelf-wall contact.
Five Rethink failures briefly contact the brick or can but leave it on the
table while the hand completes its trajectory. In the GP-only Rethink/brick
success, replay instead loses the carried brick while closure remains commanded.
Because executor timing and acquisition gates differ, this comparison does not
isolate the cause of that loss.

The Robotiq 2F-140/meat GP failure occurs during approach, before target contact,
with finger-to-shelf contact despite an accepted sampled task path. A separate
pinned, non-executing diagnostic also accepts all 103 sampled joint-interpolation
entry poses and restores the simulator state. Thus even that sampled entry check
does not validate the physically executed connection. Geometry coverage and the
difference between checked interpolation and controlled motion remain possible
explanations, not established causes. Diagnostic reproductions and checks do
not enter the 120-cell denominator. Opening during the final observation of a
stalled run must not be mistaken for completion of its policy release phase.

\subsection{Scope and limitations}
The object set, rest orientations, source trajectory and workspace bounds were
chosen during development; only the six evaluation configurations and their
grasp draws are fresh. The 120 cells share those six configurations and therefore
are not 120 independent draws from an unrestricted environment distribution.
The reported rate establishes performance in this local rigid-object domain,
with full target scans and simulator-derived object poses. It does not establish
transfer to other robot arms, an observed-only perception pipeline, physical
robots, or the full YCB collection. The GP-reference executor uses measured
tracking feedback for phase progress but queries its reference state spatially;
transported compliance is not executed by the fixed joint-position controller.
